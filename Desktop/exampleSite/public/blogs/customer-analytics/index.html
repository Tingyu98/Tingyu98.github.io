SaaS Customer Lifecycle Analytics & Dashboards

View on GitHub

Business Goals

The SaaS company aims to improve conversion, retention, and long-term customer value while supporting future product growth strategies.

However, the business lacks clear visibility into customer engagement and drop-off behavior across the subscription journey, making it difficult to optimize onboarding and retention efforts.

This project applies funnel analysis, cohort retention analysis, and customer lifetime value (CLV) analysis to identify conversion bottlenecks, retention trends, and high-value customer segments that can support product and business decision-making.

Executive Summary

The analysis identified major user drop-off during onboarding and payment stages, while retention declined noticeably between Month 3 and Month 7, revealing a key churn period in the customer lifecycle.

Champions and Loyal Customers generated over 50% of total revenue, while At Risk customers still contributed significant revenue, highlighting strong retention opportunities.

Among acquisition channels, LinkedIn Ads and Social Media attracted higher-value customers with stronger CLV performance, while Enterprise customers generated the highest long-term customer value.

The results suggest opportunities to simplify onboarding flows, improve retention during the M3–M7 period, and prioritize high-performing acquisition channels and high-value customer segments.

Dataset Structure

The dataset includes customer, subscription, revenue, and event-level tables used to analyze user behavior across the SaaS subscription lifecycle.

Key Insights

The interactive dashboards can be found on Tableau Public. Funnel / CLV & RFM

Funnel Analysis

  • Overall conversion from homepage visit to paid subscription was only 2.58%
  • The largest drop-off occurred before users reached the pricing page (64.31% drop-off)
  • Another major bottleneck appeared during the payment stage, where 65.92% of trial users did not add payment information
  • The results suggest opportunities to improve onboarding, pricing page engagement, and payment-stage user experience

Dashboard Preview

This dashboard focuses on user conversion performance across the SaaS onboarding funnel, including conversion rates, drop-off behavior, traffic distribution, and acquisition source analysis.

Cohort Retention

  • Earlier 2024 cohorts showed stronger long-term retention compared to newer 2025 cohorts
  • Retention for many cohorts dropped below 50% between Month 3 and Month 7, indicating a key churn period in the customer lifecycle

CLV & RFM Segmentation

CLV was calculated using cumulative subscription revenue generated by each customer during the analysis period minus customer acquisition cost.

  • Champions and Loyal Customers generated over 50% of total revenue, making them the most valuable customer segments
  • At Risk customers still contributed the highest total revenue, suggesting strong retention and re-engagement opportunities
  • LinkedIn Ads attracted the highest-value customers based on average customer lifetime value (CLV)
  • Enterprise plan customers dominated the top CLV segment and generated the strongest long-term revenue contribution

Recommendations

Onboarding Optimization: Simplify onboarding and pricing page flows to reduce major drop-off before signup and payment stages

Retention & Re-engagement: Launch targeted email campaigns, renewal reminders, or promotions during the Month 3–Month 7 churn window, especially for high-value At Risk customers

High-Value Customer Strategy: Prioritize retention and upsell strategies for Champions and Loyal Customers, which generated over 50% of total revenue

Acquisition Channel Optimization: Increase investment in LinkedIn Ads and Social media, which attracted higher-value customers with stronger CLV performance

Enterprise Growth Strategy: Focus on Enterprise customer acquisition and retention, as Enterprise users generated the highest long-term customer value

Google Ads Evaluation: Reevaluate Google Ads targeting and campaign quality, as it generated the lowest average CLV among acquisition channels

RFM Segmentation Logic

Customers were segmented using RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis.

  • Recency (R): how recently a customer made a payment
  • Frequency (F): how frequently a customer made transactions
  • Monetary (M): customer lifetime value (CLV) and overall revenue contribution

Each metric was scored from 1 to 5 using NTILE(5) window functions.

Customer segments were then assigned using business rules:

  • Champions: high recency, frequency, and monetary scores
  • Loyal Customers: consistently active and high-value customers
  • New / Promising: recent customers with growth potential
  • At Risk: previously active customers showing declining engagement
  • Lost Customers: inactive customers with low engagement and value
